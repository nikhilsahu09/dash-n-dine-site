Engineering & Development

How Dash N Dine is being built, tested, and improved.

Technical overview

Turning a game idea into a working system

Dash N Dine combines a 3D city environment with driving and delivery gameplay. Development involves building the world, organizing scenes and scripts, and connecting gameplay systems so that the different parts work together.

01 / ENVIRONMENT

Building the city

The city is assembled from roads, sidewalks, buildings, and supporting scene objects. The goal is to create a world that feels organized and gives the player room to navigate.

02 / NAVIGATION

Roads & waypoints

Waypoints help represent possible paths through the road network. Connecting them correctly is important for route planning and helping the player reach the intended destination instead of simply heading toward the nearest point.

Testing focuses on how paths connect, whether turns are handled correctly, and whether the chosen route reaches the intended location.

03 / GAMEPLAY

Driving & vehicle selection

The game includes a vehicle-selection workflow and a driving experience. A key development task is ensuring the selected vehicle is the one that actually appears when gameplay begins.

Gameplay testing helps catch mismatches between menus, selected vehicles, and the active game scene.

04 / DEBUGGING

Testing & troubleshooting

Development includes investigating issues, checking scene behavior, and testing exported builds. Web deployment also requires checking compatibility and performance in browsers.

When something breaks, the process is to reproduce the issue, inspect the relevant code or project settings, make a targeted change, and test again.

Development principles

Build, test, improve

Modular scenesGDScriptDebuggingIterative testing

Rather than treating the game as one large system, development is approached as a series of smaller tasks: build a feature, connect it to the rest of the project, test the result, and refine it when problems appear.

This page describes the development areas and challenges involved in the project. It does not claim that every feature or technical issue is fully finished.
